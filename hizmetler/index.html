Step by step · The quote routine

How one routine moves into the system

The numbers are an example; yours come out of the analysis. The logic is the same for every routine: measure, build, gain, scale.

  1. 01Day 1

    Analysis: 6 quotes a day, 25 minutes each

    Each one typed by hand from an old spreadsheet: price list, currency, lead time. 12 hours a week, plus the typos.

  2. 02Weeks 1–2

    Build: request in, quote out

    The request arrives by form or email. The system pulls the price, fills the template, makes the PDF and sends it. You look only at the unusual ones.

  3. 03From week 3

    Gain: 2 minutes instead of 25. 12 hours a week back.

    About 48 hours a month, a quarter of one person's time. Zero copy-paste errors; follow-up runs on its own.

  4. 04Ongoing

    Scale: three times the quotes, same team

    When the work grows, the hours don't. The time you get back goes into finding customers.

02 — Proforma invoice automationOrder → proforma → payment

The proforma is ready before you open Excel.

For exporters and wholesalers the proforma is the document that closes the deal: a wrong currency, a missing payment term or a day's delay costs the order. The system prepares it from the quote or the order, sends it, follows the payment and logs it in accounting. You approve.

The flow · From order to payment

What happens when an order comes in?

Whichever channel the customer writes on, the path is the same: order, proforma, approval, payment and accounting are one chain. You're only the approval link.

  1. Customer

    Asks for a quote or places an order.

    Email, WhatsApp, form or B2B portal

    within minutes
