01 — Proforma invoice automationOrder → proforma → approval → sent

  1. 1 · Customer

    The order arrives by email.

    Your customer writes the way they always do: what, how many, by when. No new form to fill in.

  2. 2 · Customer

    Or in a spreadsheet.

    Product code, quantity, requested delivery date. Excel or CSV, the format doesn't matter. Hundreds of lines, and nobody types them in again.

  3. 3 · AI

    It reads and prepares the proforma.

    It works out what was ordered, then checks your stock system and price list: that customer's price, with or without tax, by country. Then it fills in the proforma.

  4. 4 · AI

    It prepares the packing list too.

    Ready alongside the proforma: cartons and weight. It knows your carton sizes too. You don't sell 60 from a carton of 72; it spots that and tells you.

  5. 5 · You

    It asks: do you approve?

    “This email came in, this is what they want, here's the invoice.” You look it over and, if it's right, press approve. That's it.

  6. 6 · You

    If something's off, you say so.

    Press edit and write what should change in a normal sentence. It prepares the proforma again and asks once more: shall I send it?

  7. 7 · AI

    After you approve, it sends.

    The proforma and packing list go to the customer's inbox. Nothing goes out until you approve it.

Step by step · The quote routine

How one routine moves into the system

The numbers are an example; yours come out of the analysis. The logic is the same for every routine: measure, build, gain, scale.

  1. 01Day 1

    Analysis: 6 quotes a day, 25 minutes each

    Each one typed by hand from an old spreadsheet: price list, currency, lead time. 12 hours a week, plus the typos.

  2. 02Weeks 1–2

    Build: request in, quote out

    The request arrives by form or email. The system pulls the price, fills the template and makes the PDF. You check and approve; the system sends it.

  3. 03From week 3

    Gain: 2 minutes instead of 25. 12 hours a week back.

    About 48 hours a month, a quarter of one person's time. Zero copy-paste errors; follow-up runs on its own.

  4. 04Ongoing

    Scale: three times the quotes, same team

    When the work grows, the hours don't. The time you get back goes into finding customers.

05 · Invoice and bank checkInvoices · bank statements · every location

Invoices against your bank. Every gap on one list.

For restaurants, cafés, bakeries and pubs with 2 to 30 locations. We take your supplier invoices and bank statements, match them, and list what is missing or does not add up, each item with where we found it. Your accountant gets a clean table to check. We do not replace your accountant and we do not give tax advice.

We start with past years, then keep the same routine every month. If you want, a panel can show all your locations on one screen.

  • 01Invoices matched to bank payments→Email · folders · photos · statements
  • 02Missing and inconsistent items, with sources→Paid twice · no invoice · amount differs
  • 03Till takings against what reached the bank→Till report · delivery app payouts
  • 04A clean table for your accountant→One file · every location · past years first
